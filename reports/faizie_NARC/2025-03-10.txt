At the **germination stage (BBCH 00)** of wheat, your focus should be on creating optimal conditions for seedling emergence and early root development. Here’s what you need to do:

### **Irrigation**
- Ensure the soil is consistently moist but not waterlogged. Overwatering can lead to poor germination or seedling diseases.
- Water lightly every 2–3 days, depending on your soil type and local rainfall. Use drip irrigation or furrow methods to maintain uniform moisture without puddling.

### **Fertilizer Application**
- Apply a **starter fertilizer** rich in phosphorus (P) to promote root development and energy transfer during germination.
- DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate) is ideal, as it provides both nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P). Use 20–30 kg of DAP per hectare as a basal dressing at sowing.
- Avoid excessive nitrogen application at this stage, as it can lead to delayed germination or lodging later in the season.

### **Pesticides/Insecticides**
- Monitor for any signs of pests like aphids, mites, or fungal diseases (e.g., damping-off). If detected early, apply a systemic pesticide/insecticide targeting the specific pest.
- Use neem oil or other biopesticides as a precautionary measure to suppress pests and diseases without harming beneficial insects.

### **Other Actions**
1. **Soil Health**:
   - Ensure soil temperature is optimal for germination (ideally 15–20°C for wheat).
   - Test soil pH and ensure it’s within the range of 6.0–7.5 for wheat.
   
2. **Mulching**:
   - Apply a thin layer of mulch (e.g., crop residues) to retain soil moisture, suppress weeds, and regulate soil temperature.

3. **Avoid Compaction**:
   - Ensure the soil is not compacted, as it can hinder root penetration and water infiltration.
